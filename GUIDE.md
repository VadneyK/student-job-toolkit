# The guide, in plain words

This kit is for college students and new grads looking for an internship or a first job, and for the working adults in their community who want to help. It costs nothing. It works with a free Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini account. You need no API key, no paid plan, and no app to install.

It does not apply for you, and it will not make anything up for you. It helps you do six things well: turn what you have already done (church service, campus ministry, tutoring, part-time work) into honest resume lines, know what you can go after, skip jobs you cannot get, spot scams, ask the right person for help, and show your work.

## Start in 10 minutes

1. **Open the kit page.** Download `kit-page/index.html` and open it in your phone or laptop browser. It works offline and keeps everything on your device. Bookmark it.
2. **Log 2 things you did (4 minutes).** On the **Experience** tab, add two things from this year: a role in your campus ministry, a church event you helped run, tutoring, a part-time job, a class project. Say what you did, one action per line, with real numbers where you have them. The **Resume lines** tab turns them into resume lines with no AI, and tells you how to make each one stronger.
3. **Make your profile card (4 minutes).** On the **Prompts** tab, tap **Copy with my card** on prompt 1. Open a new free chat, paste, and replace `[PASTE YOUR BACKGROUND HERE]` with your resume text (or your new resume lines). Paste the filled card it gives back into the **Card** tab. Fix anything that is not true, and answer every ASK ME yourself.
4. **Check one real job post (2 minutes).** Copy prompt 2, paste it into a new chat, and paste the post's text where it says. You get 5 checks and a verdict: apply, skip, or stop.

After that: a fit check before every application, a scam check on every job message you did not ask for, one ask to a person in your field, and a weekly review.

No kit page? Every prompt is also a plain file in `prompts/`. Keep your card in a phone note and paste it where a prompt says `[PASTE YOUR PROFILE CARD HERE]`.

## Your service is experience

Leading a small group, running a retreat registration desk, tutoring, setting up for Sunday, managing a sign-up sheet: these are real experience, and most students leave them off. The Experience tab is where you record them while you still remember the details: your role, the dates, the hours, what you did, what you made, and who it reached.

Describe each thing as what it was. Name the group honestly ("campus ministry", or its real name). Let the actions and numbers do the work: "Led a team of 6 volunteers at check-in" says more than "passionate servant leader". If a number is a guess, tick "estimate" and the line will say "About".

If an adult saw your work (a staff member, a pastor, a ministry leader), ask them to confirm the entry. The Resume lines tab writes the message for you. If they confirm, ask whether they would be a reference. A reference who watched you work is worth more than one who only knows your name. Ask before you list anyone.

## The ideas behind it

**Skip what you cannot get, tailor the rest.** More applications do raise your chances of an interview, so keep applying. The fit check is for cutting the roles you would fail on level, eligibility, or papers, so your time goes to roles you can get, each with a short note that names one real detail from the post.

**Watch the student traps.** The fit check looks hardest at what trips up students: graduation dates and eligibility rules, work authorization, and posts titled "entry level" that quietly require 3 years. Unknown is an honest answer; a guessed Pass is not.

**Check for scams first.** Real employers do not send you a check to buy equipment, ask for bank details before an offer, or interview only over a chat app or text. Prompt 3 checks the signs and gives one of three answers: likely scam, check first, or looks real.

**Ask a person in your field.** The most useful help is 20 minutes with someone who does the work you want. Most people are glad to give it when the ask is short and easy to decline. Ask for a referral only for a role your fit check says you fit; then the person vouching for you is vouching for something true.

**Show, do not just tell.** For the one or two roles you want most, build one small piece of the actual work from public information, write down every decision, and list the questions only the company could answer (prompt 6). It goes next to your short note, not in place of it.

**Tell the truth.** Every prompt tells the chat to use only what you gave it and to label guesses. Keep your facts the same everywhere: dates, GPA, language levels, skill levels. If a company says no AI tools on a test or take-home, follow that. A gap you are honest about costs you less than a claim that falls apart in an interview.

## Free plan tips

- Start a new chat for each prompt. Long chats on free plans get slow and forget the start.
- If the answer stops halfway, type `continue`.
- If you hit the free limit, come back later or switch to another free chat. Your card and log stay on your device.
- Paste text, not links. Free chats often cannot open links, and many job pages block them anyway.
- Your card holds no phone, address, or ID numbers, so it is safe to paste. Most free chats let you turn off training on your chats in settings if you prefer.
- Never paste other people's details (a helper's email, a reference's phone number) into a chat.

## Your data

The kit page stores everything in your browser on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere. Browsers can clear saved pages (private windows, clearing site data, some phones), so download a backup each week from the **Backup** tab. To move to a new device, open the kit page there and restore the backup file.

## For communities

If you are part of a church, campus ministry, or alumni group, the `community/` folder shows how to connect students with working members in their field: a helper opt-in form, a private student request form, a coordinator guide, and a facilitated 4 to 6 evening job club. No app and no new software.
