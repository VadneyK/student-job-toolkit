# 3. Sort a recruiter message and draft the reply

When: a person writes to you about a job (email, LinkedIn, Handshake, a text), or you met a recruiter at a career fair.
Time: 3 minutes.

The rule this prompt follows: ask the few questions that decide fit before you book a call. Pay first, then place, then process. You do not name your number first. You never book a call for a role that already fails one check. And you check for scams before you click anything.

## Paste this into a new chat

```text
Help me sort one message about a job and draft my reply. Use only the message and my card. Never guess a Pass.

My profile card:
[PASTE YOUR PROFILE CARD HERE]

The message (paste all of it, including the sender's name, email address, or profile headline):
[PASTE THE MESSAGE HERE]

1. Kind: a real person about a real role / job alert or newsletter / interview scheduling / rejection / offer / possible scam.
2. Scam check. List every sign you see: money, equipment, or bank details before an offer; interview only by chat app; an offer with no interview; a free email address for a company; the sender's company, title, or the role changes between messages; odd spelling or mixed alphabets in names; pressure to act today; a link or file to open before anything is confirmed. If any: write "Do not click links or open files. Look the company up yourself." and stop there.
3. If it is a role, score 5 checks as Pass, Fail, or Unknown, quoting the line used: role fit, level, pay, place and papers, process.
4. Pick the reply: A (questions first) if no Fail and some Unknown. B (yes, let's talk) only if all 5 Pass. C (kind no thanks) if any Fail. None for alerts, newsletters, and scams.
5. Write that reply in under 90 words, plain and friendly, signed with my first name:
   - A asks only the Unknowns, at most 3, in this order: pay, place, process, which company (if a staffing agency did not say), level. For process, ask: "What are the interview steps? If there's a take-home, about how long is it, and what's the policy on AI tools?"
   - Never state my pay number. If they asked for it, say I'd like to hear the range for the role first.
   - Staffing agency: add "Please don't submit my profile anywhere until we've talked."
   - B offers 3 times I am free as [TIME 1], [TIME 2], [TIME 3], or my scheduling link if my card has one.
   - C names the first failing check kindly with no numbers, and says I'd be glad to hear about roles that fit my target roles.
6. Tracker line: next step and a date.
```

## What you should get back

The kind, any scam signs, a 5-row score, one reply under 90 words, and a next step. Read the reply out loud before you send it. Change anything that does not sound like you.

## The three replies, as plain templates

If the chat is unavailable, fill these by hand.

A. Questions first

```text
Hi [name],

Thanks for reaching out. This could be a fit, and I'd like to learn a bit more before we set up a call:
1. What is the pay range for the role?
2. Is it remote, hybrid, or onsite, and where?
3. What are the interview steps? If there's a take-home, about how long is it, and what's the policy on AI tools?

Thanks,
[your first name]
```

B. Yes, let's talk

```text
Hi [name],

Thanks for the details. This sounds like a real match: [one reason from their answers].

I'd be glad to talk. I'm free [TIME 1], [TIME 2], or [TIME 3]. My resume is attached.

Thanks,
[your first name]
```

C. Kind no thanks

```text
Hi [name],

Thanks for thinking of me. This one isn't a fit for me: [reason, no numbers].

If you have [your target roles] roles later, I'd be glad to hear about them.

Thanks,
[your first name]
```

Reason lines for C: "the range is below where I need to be"; "I'm looking for roles in [place] or remote"; "it asks for more years than I have"; "it's further from my background than would be useful to you".

## Why these rules

- Asking pay, place, and process by email first is normal. Engineers and recruiters both recommend it, and many US states (California, Colorado, New York, Washington, Illinois among them) require employers to share a pay range in posts or on request.
- Agencies are paid when they place someone, so some submit profiles fast. The "don't submit" line keeps you in control of where your name goes.
- The AI question is honest, not a trick. If a company says no AI on the take-home, you follow that.
