# 1. Build your role profile

When: first, once. Again after a new job, internship, or big project.
Time: 5 minutes.

Most students apply to whatever title they saw last. This prompt reads everything you have done and tells you, with evidence, which kinds of jobs you can honestly go after now, which are a stretch, and what to fix in how you describe yourself.

## Paste this into a new chat

```text
You are helping a college student or new grad see which jobs they can honestly go after now. Rules: use only the facts I paste. Never invent experience, numbers, or skills. Label anything you infer as "inference". If you do not know, write "unknown".

My background (resume text, classes, projects, jobs, clubs, volunteering, anything):
[PASTE YOUR BACKGROUND HERE]

What I think I want (a sentence, or "not sure"):
[ANSWER HERE]

Do these steps, in order:
1. List 5 to 8 role families I could apply to now, with the job titles employers use for each.
2. For each family give: the evidence from my background (quote it), my level (intern, entry level, or stretch), and a strength: Strong (direct, recent evidence), Moderate (real but thin), or Weak (would not survive one focused interview question).
3. One line each: how it fits what I said I want.
4. Claims to fix: any fact I state two different ways, any claim bigger than its evidence, any number with no source. Give the honest version.
5. For my top 2 families: the one skill or proof piece that would most raise my strength, and a free way to get it in 2 weeks.
6. Fill in my profile card below. Leave a field blank if I did not give you the fact, and write ASK ME where you need my answer.

[PASTE YOUR PROFILE CARD HERE]

Format: a table for steps 1 to 3, then short lines. Plain words.
```

## What you should get back

A table of role families with quoted evidence and a strength for each, a short "claims to fix" list, two gap fixes, and a filled card. Save the card in the kit page's Card tab (or a phone note) and answer every ASK ME yourself.

## If it goes wrong

- It praises everything as Strong: reply "Be stricter. Which of these would fail one focused interview question?"
- It adds a skill you do not have: reply "Remove anything I did not write myself." The card must be true. Recruiters check, and a false line costs more than a gap.
