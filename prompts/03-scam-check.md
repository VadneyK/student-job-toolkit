# 3. Scam check on a message

When: any email, text, Handshake, or LinkedIn message about a job that you did not ask for, before you click anything or reply.
Time: 1 minute.

Fake job offers aimed at students are common: "data entry, 40 dollars an hour, no interview", a check to buy equipment, a "hiring manager" on a chat app. This prompt checks the signs before you click a link, open a file, or send your details.

## Paste this into a new chat

```text
Check this job message for scam signs before I click anything or reply. Use only what is in the message. Be specific and calm.

My profile card (for context only):
[PASTE YOUR PROFILE CARD HERE]

The message (paste all of it, including the sender's name, email address or phone number, and any links as text):
[PASTE THE MESSAGE HERE]

1. Kind: a real person about a real role / job alert or newsletter / interview scheduling for a job I applied to / rejection / offer / likely scam / not sure.
2. Signs. Check each and say yes or no, quoting the line: money, a check, gift cards, or bank details asked before a real offer; buying equipment from "their vendor"; interview only by text or chat app; an offer with no interview; pay far above normal for simple work; a free email address (gmail, outlook, and so on) for a company; a sender whose company, title, or role does not match or changes; odd spelling or mixed alphabets in names; pressure to act today; a link or file to open before anything is confirmed; asks for my address, ID, or Social Security number early.
3. Verdict: LIKELY SCAM, CHECK FIRST, or LOOKS REAL, with the two strongest reasons.
4. What to do next, in 3 steps or fewer. For LIKELY SCAM or CHECK FIRST: do not click links or open files; find the company's real website by searching its name yourself; contact them through that site; report the message to the platform it came through.
5. If it looks real and is about a job, say "Run prompt 2 on the job post next." Do not write a reply.
```

## What you should get back

A verdict with the reasons, quoted, and at most 3 steps. If you already clicked a link or sent details, tell your school's career center and change any password you typed.

## Where to report

- The platform it came through (Handshake, LinkedIn, and job boards all have a report button).
- In the US: reportfraud.ftc.gov. If money was lost: ic3.gov.
