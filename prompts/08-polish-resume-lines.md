# 8. Turn what you did into resume lines

When: you have done things that are not on your resume yet (church service, campus ministry, events, tutoring, projects, part-time work), or the kit page's Resume tab already wrote lines and you want them tighter.
Time: 5 minutes.

Most students leave service and campus work off their resume, and it is real experience. Two things make it count with employers: lead with the role you held (employers weigh leadership far above "volunteer"), and show each skill with an example. One more thing worth knowing: in US field tests, resumes with a religious signal got about a quarter fewer replies. So this prompt flags any line that names a church or ministry and offers an accurate neutral version. Which one you use is your choice. Never describe a group as something it was not, and keep the church's name for faith-based employers.

Use the kit page's Resume tab if you have it: it writes the lines with no AI. If not, a rough list is fine here.

## Paste this into a new chat

```text
Turn my experience into resume lines for a student or new grad. Rules: keep every fact; add no facts, tools, numbers, or results I did not write. Keep "About" on any number I call an estimate. Do not turn a team or event result into something I did alone: if a number belongs to the team or the event, say so. Lead each entry with the role I held. Each line starts with a strong verb (past tense for finished work, present tense for ongoing) and stays under 20 words.

My profile card:
[PASTE YOUR PROFILE CARD HERE]

My experience (lines from the kit page, or a rough list: what it was, my role, when, about how many hours, what I did, any real numbers, who saw it):
[PASTE YOUR EXPERIENCE LINES HERE]

A job post I am aiming at (optional; write NONE if there is none):
[PASTE THE JOB POST HERE, OR WRITE NONE]

1. For each entry: a header line (role, what it was, group, dates, hours), then 1 to 3 lines. If I gave lines already, show old and new.
2. Skills with an example: list up to 6 skills, each with the one line that proves it.
3. Neutral wording: for every line that names a church, ministry, or religious event, give a neutral version that is still accurate (for example "student organization" for a campus ministry, "nonprofit youth program" for a church youth group, "weekend retreat for 200 students" for a named retreat). Show both; I will choose.
4. Mark any line you could not improve without inventing something, and give me one question to ask myself that would make it stronger (for example: "How many people came?").
5. If I gave a job post: pick the 3 lines that matter most for it, quoting the line of the post each one answers.
6. For each entry, the person I could ask to be a reference: the adult who led or saw the work (a role, not a name), and a 2-line message asking them to confirm the entry and be a reference, easy to say no to.
```

## If it goes wrong

- It adds a tool or number you did not write: reply "Remove anything I did not write myself."
- It drops "About" from an estimate: reply "Keep About on estimated numbers."
- It credits you with the whole team's numbers ("Processed 180 check-ins" when your team did): reply "That was the team. Say so."
- Its neutral version changes what the group was ("a nonprofit company" for a church): reply "Accurate, please: what kind of group was it really?"
