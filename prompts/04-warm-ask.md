# 4. Ask someone in your field for help

When: you found a person who works in your field (through your church, campus ministry, club, alumni group, or a coordinator who matched you), and you want a short call, a resume look, a mock interview, or, for a role you fit, a referral.
Time: 3 minutes.

People who already work where you want to work are the most useful help there is, and most are glad to give 20 minutes. The ask that works is short, specific, and easy to say no to. Asking for a referral is fair only when your fit check (prompt 2) says APPLY; then the helper is vouching for something true.

## Paste this into a new chat

```text
Help me write a short message asking someone in my field for help. Use only the facts I give. Under 80 words. One clear ask. Warm, plain, not flattering, and easy for them to say no to. Signed with my first name.

My profile card:
[PASTE YOUR PROFILE CARD HERE]

Who they are and how I know of them (for example: "works as a data analyst at a hospital; we are both in the same church; the career team matched us"):
[WHO THEY ARE]

What I am asking for (pick one: a 20-minute call / a look at my resume / a mock interview / a referral for one role):
[MY ASK]

If I am asking about one role, my fit check result for it (paste the verdict and table from prompt 2):
[PASTE THE FIT CHECK HERE, OR WRITE NONE]

1. If my ask is a referral and the fit check is not APPLY, or there is none: say so, and write the message as a 20-minute call ask instead.
2. Write the message. Say how I know of them in one line, what I am studying or doing in one line, the one ask, and offer 2 or 3 evening or weekend times as [TIME 1], [TIME 2], [TIME 3]. End with a line that makes no easy: "If now's not a good time, no worries at all."
3. Write a 2-line thank-you I can send after, with a [ONE THING I LEARNED] slot.
4. Write a 2-line follow-up for one week later if they do not answer. After that, I let it go.
```

## What good looks like

- Under 80 words. They can answer from a phone in a minute.
- One ask, not three.
- A referral ask names the one role and says you checked your fit honestly. Never ask someone to refer you to a role you would fail.
- Thank them the same day, and tell them later how it turned out. People help again when they hear it mattered.
