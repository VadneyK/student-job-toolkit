# 5. Get ready for a mock interview with someone in your field

When: a member, alum, or mentor in your field agreed to run a practice interview with you.
Time: 5 minutes to prepare, then the call.

A practice interview with someone who does the job is worth more than ten with a general coach: they know what their team actually asks. This prompt gets you ready, writes what to send them ahead, and gives you what to ask after.

## Paste this into a new chat

```text
Help me prepare for a practice interview with someone who works in my field. Use only my card and the post. Label anything you guess as "guess". Do not invent experience for me.

My profile card:
[PASTE YOUR PROFILE CARD HERE]

The job post I am practicing for (or the kind of role, if there is no post):
[PASTE THE JOB POST HERE]

What my helper does (one line):
[WHAT THEY DO]

1. A message to send them 2 days ahead, under 80 words: thanks, the post or role, my resume is attached, and the one area I most want practice on.
2. The 8 questions this kind of interview most likely asks: 3 about my background, 3 about the work itself (technical or case questions for this field), 2 about how I work with people. Mark which ones the post points to, quoting the line.
3. For each of my 3 strongest evidence lines on the card: a 4-sentence story I could tell (what was the situation, what I did, what happened, what I learned). Use only what is on the card; where a number is my estimate, keep the word "estimate".
4. The 2 places in my card a real interviewer is most likely to push on, and an honest way to answer.
5. Three questions to ask my helper at the end, for example: "What would make you hesitate to hire me?", "What does a strong answer to the work question sound like on your team?", "What should I learn first?"
6. A thank-you message for after, under 60 words, with a [ONE THING I WILL CHANGE] slot.
```

## After the call

- Write down their answers to your three questions the same day. That is the real value.
- Send the thank-you the same day.
- If they offered more help, ask for one thing at a time.
- If a company asks you to take a test or a take-home with no AI tools, practice without them too.
